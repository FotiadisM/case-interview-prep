# The Big 3 Compared

All three firms test the same core skills — structuring, quant, insight, synthesis, communication — but the format, the screen before the interview, and the cultural emphasis differ. Practice the firm's format, not just the case types.

| Dimension | McKinsey | BCG | Bain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Live case style** | Interviewer-led | Candidate-led, exhibit-heavy | Candidate-led (partners) + standardized interviewer-led first rounds in many offices |
| **Online screen** | Solve game (Redrock + Sea Wolf modules) | Casey chatbot (+ CCA / Pymetrics at some offices) | SOVA / TestGorilla / HireVue by region |
| **Fit component** | PEI — structured deep-dive, ~50% of score | Conversational fit woven through interviews | Experience interview (10–15 min), collaborative tone |
| **Rounds** | 2 rounds (2 + 2–3 interviews) | 2 rounds (2 + 2–3), written case at select offices | 2–3 rounds (2 + 2–3), written case at select offices |
| **Interview feel** | Structured, rapid, rigorous | Intellectual, data-driven, creative | Warm, collaborative, "Answer First" |
| **Culture shorthand** | Leadership, personal impact, values | Intellectual curiosity, creativity | Results, teamwork, "smart, nice, gets things done" |
| **Case-type lean** | More public sector + ops; sizing embedded in cases | Exhibit-heavy; creative/lateral thinking rewarded | More PE/DD cases (Bain Capital connection) |
| **Acceptance** | under 1% of applicants overall at each firm | under 1% | under 1% |

## The one adjustment that costs candidates the most

**Interviewer-led vs candidate-led.** At McKinsey, the interviewer assigns the next question and evaluates each answer independently — so practice crisp standalone answers (quant, exhibits, brainstorming) anchored to a clear structure. At BCG/Bain, you own the agenda — the silence after your framework is an invitation to drive, not to wait. But note the 2026 nuance: several sources report Bain stands stretched first-round interviews to standardized interviewer-led cases (and BCG remains candidate-led) — so if you interview at multiple firms, practice both styles regardless of legacy reputation.

## Behavior differences that show up in cases

- **McKinsey** expects you to answer *their* questions with structure — each response is a mini-synthesis, not a preamble.
- **BCG** feeds you exhibits constantly and rewards concise chart→driver→implication reading plus creative brainstorming quality.
- **Bain** wants the hypothesis early and the answer first — say where you're going before you walk there.

## How to split your prep across firms

1. Master the shared skills first: profitability + market entry + market sizing + clean math (they're universal).
2. Then drill each firm's format with 3–5 firm-style cases.
3. Prepare firm-specific fit in parallel: PEI stories (McKinsey), conversational examples + curiosity signals (BCG), experience stories + collaborative anecdotes (Bain).
4. Practice the right screen: Solve simulators, Casey-style chatbot cases, or SOVA practice.
