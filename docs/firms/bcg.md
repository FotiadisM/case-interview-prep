# Boston Consulting Group (BCG)

BCG's hallmark is the **candidate-led, exhibit-heavy case**: you drive the case from start to finish while the interviewer feeds exhibits and probes your reasoning. The firm selects for intellectual curiosity, creativity, and data-driven rigor.

Source: [CaseStar – BCG guide](https://www.casestar.io/guides/bcg), [HackingTheCaseInterview – BCG prep](https://www.hackingthecaseinterview.com/pages/bcg-case-interview-prep), [Road to Offer – BCG guide](https://www.roadtooffer.com/blog/bcg-case-interview-guide)

## The process

| Stage | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Application | Resume screening; strong academics + genuine intellectual interests |
| 2. Online screens | **Casey chatbot** (most offices); some offices use the Consulting Career Assessment (CCA) and/or Pymetrics instead of or alongside |
| 3. First round | 2 interviews ~45 min: 10–15 min fit + 25–30 min candidate-led case |
| 4. Final round | 2–3 interviews with partners (often more creative/lateral; some offices add a **written case**) |
| 5. Offer | Glassdoor: ~30–40% of first-round candidates reach finals; ~15–30% of finalists get offers |

## The candidate-led case

**You own the agenda.** Present your structure, state the hypothesis, choose which branch to explore, request the data you need, interpret the exhibits, and drive to a recommendation — the interviewer mostly answers your questions and supplies exhibits. The silence after your framework is a test: proceed with confidence, signposting every step.

BCG's distinctive pressure points:

- **Exhibits, exhibits, exhibits.** BCG cases are the most chart-heavy of the big 3. Master the 30-second exhibit read: (1) what the chart says, (2) the driver it reveals, (3) the implication for the client. Road to Offer's recommended flow is "describe the chart, interpret the driver, then state the implication" — this exact move is scored.
- **Short, sharp synthesis.** BCG rewards concise 3–5 sentence answers and written-style precision even in speech.
- **Creative/lateral thinking** — brainstorming quality matters here more visibly than elsewhere; structured creativity (bucketed ideas) beats quantity.
- **Quant breadth** — percentages, breakeven, payback, margin and sizing math must be automatic, no calculator.

## The Casey chatbot (online case)

A 25–35 minute chatbot-led business case, 6–10 sequential questions (structuring, exhibit interpretation, quant, brainstorm, judgment), **no going back**, often ending with a **60-second recorded video recommendation** (timed, no retake). Select offices use the CCA instead (30 min: behavioral + 12-min math/logic) and some run Pymetrics. Unofficial pass estimates hover around 20–30%; don't anchor prep on funnel rumors (Road to Offer) — prepare for the format.

Casey prep = case prep done alone, under time: practice timed exhibit questions, written 3–4 sentence answers, mental math, and rehearse delivering a 60-second answer-first recommendation on camera (twice a week is plenty; the format shock is the real risk).

## The written case (select offices)

More common in Europe/Asia. You receive a packet (15–30 pages of data), have limited time to analyze, and present a recommendation + Q&A. Tests data synthesis and written structure. Calculator allowed. Preparation: practice reading a slide deck and producing a one-page structured answer with a clear recommendation, then defending it.

## Culture, focus areas & fit at BCG

- **What the firm emphasizes:** intellectual curiosity (they screen for genuine interests — your "hobbies" answers matter), hypothesis-driven rigor, teamwork ("we score and value team play highly"), BCG X for digital/build work.
- **Fit signals that help:** asking sharp questions (BCG interviewers often note the quality of your questions), a curious mind across domains, case leadership without arrogance, genuine motivation grounded in specifics of BCG's work or culture.
- **Fit format:** conversational, woven through each interview — not a separate PEI. Expect "why consulting, why BCG, tell me about a time…" in a friendly but probing style.
- **What to avoid:** memorized enthusiasm, unquantified stories, letting the case drift (in candidate-led, drift = death).

## Firm-specific prep checklist (BCG)

1. Practice driving: run candidate-led mocks where your partner roleplays a passive interviewer you must engage.
2. Drill exhibits daily for a week — 30-second reads, then state the implication.
3. Do timed math drills (no calculator) — BCG quant is unforgiving.
4. Rehearse the Casey sequence: structured answer → exhibit → math → brainstorm under a ticking clock, video recommendation included.
5. Prepare 6–8 conversational fit stories with numbers, plus thoughtful questions for your interviewers.
6. Use BCG's official [case interview preparation](https://careers.bcg.com/global/en/case-interview-preparation) materials and interactive case.
