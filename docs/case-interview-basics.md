# Case Interview Basics

A case interview is a 30–45 minute conversation in which you solve a realistic business problem out loud while the interviewer scores how you think. This page covers what is actually scored, the universal solving loop, and the communication habits that separate passing candidates from failing ones.

## What interviewers actually score

Across McKinsey, BCG, and Bain, interviewers evaluate roughly the same five dimensions. Use these as your personal checklist after every practice case:

1. **Structuring** — Can you break an ambiguous problem into a MECE, tailored logic before diving in? This is the single highest-signal skill and is typically scored first.
2. **Analytics & math** — Can you do percent-change, breakeven, and market-split math mentally, accurately, and out loud?
3. **Insight / business judgment** — Do you distinguish what matters from what doesn't? Do you spot the driver instead of boiling the ocean?
4. **Synthesis** — Can you compress a 25-minute analysis into an answer-first, 60-second recommendation with reasons, risks, and next steps?
5. **Coachability / communication** — Do you signpost, listen to hints, adjust when data contradicts your hypothesis, and stay easy to follow?

Source: [Road to Offer – McKinsey Case Interview 2026](https://www.roadtooffer.com/blog/mckinsey-case-interview-guide), [StrategyCase – What interviewers evaluate](https://strategycase.com/consulting-case-interviews-a-comprehensive-guide/)

## The universal solving loop

Regardless of case type, every strong candidate runs the same loop:

1. **Restate & clarify (1–2 min).** Capture the objective in one sentence and ask the 2–3 scoping questions that change the answer (objective, geography, time horizon, metric of success). Misreading the objective is the most common single cause of failure.
2. **Build a tailored structure (1–2 min of quiet + presentation).** Ask for 30–60 seconds of silence. Build a 3–5 bucket issue tree from first principles, not from memory. Test it against the TRIM criteria: **T**ailored to this client, **R**elevant to the objective, **I**nsight-driven (hypotheses, not topic labels), **M**ECE.
3. **Drive the analysis (15–25 min).** State a hypothesis, pick the bucket most likely to move the answer, request the data you need, interpret exhibits, and quantify. Signpost which bucket you are in at all times ("Moving to the cost side of my tree…"). Use the 80/20 rule: chase the driver, not completeness.
4. **Synthesize & recommend (2–3 min).** Answer first, then support with 2–3 reasons, quantify the impact, name the biggest risk, and give next steps. Never end with options — end with a decision.

## Interviewer-led vs candidate-led

| | Interviewer-led (McKinsey) | Candidate-led (BCG, classic Bain) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pace & direction | Interviewer assigns sub-questions one at a time | You propose what to analyze next |
| Structuring | Shorter, focused on each question | Full case structure you drive from start to end |
| Key risk | Treating each question as disconnected instead of anchoring to your structure | Drifting — losing the thread instead of leading deliberately |
| Preview skill | Answering standalone quant/exhibit questions crisply | Proactive hypothesis-driven leadership |

Real cases today often blend the two. Even in interviewer-led cases, keep your overall tree in mind; even in candidate-led cases, be ready to answer direct questions. Source: [HackingTheCaseInterview – McKinsey prep](https://www.hackingthecaseinterview.com/pages/best-resources-to-prepare-for-mckinsey-case-interviews), [CaseStar – BCG guide](https://www.casestar.io/guides/bcg)

## The math you must do cold

- **Percent change**: new/old − 1
- **Margin math**: margin = profit/revenue; revenue = profit/margin
- **Breakeven**: fixed costs ÷ unit contribution margin (or ÷ gross margin %)
- **Market share**: your revenue ÷ market revenue
- **Payback**: investment ÷ annual savings or cash flow
- **Compound growth**: the Rule of 72 (72 ÷ growth rate % ≈ years to double)

Practice these until they feel automatic — mental math failures sink otherwise-strong candidates. Drill tools: [RocketBlocks](https://www.rocketblocks.me/consulting.php), [Road to Offer math drills](https://www.roadtooffer.com/signup?next=%2Fdrills%2Fmath).

## Communication habits to copy

- **Signpost constantly** — "I've confirmed the driver is on the revenue side, so I'll now segment revenue by product line."
- **Think out loud, but in structured bursts** — never silent-analyze for more than a few seconds in an interviewer-led exchange.
- **Sanity-check every number** — "That implies $4B revenue per store, which seems high; let me re-check my assumption."
- **Name your assumptions** — market sizing and estimation live or die by stated, reasonable assumptions.
- **Answer first** — every exhibit interpretation and final recommendation starts with the conclusion, then the support (the Pyramid Principle).
