# Market Sizing Cases

Market sizing questions are asked standalone ("How many golf balls fit in the Empire State Building?"), embedded as the opening of market entry and new product cases, and increasingly as timed drills inside interviewer-led cases. They test logical estimation, creativity, and clean mental math — not whether you know the answer.

Source: [Management Consulted – Market sizing framework](https://managementconsulted.com/market-sizing/), [MyConsultingOffer – case types](https://www.myconsultingoffer.org/case-study-interview-prep/types/)

## What the case is asking

Estimate a figure — market revenue, units, or population — where the point is a *defensible, structured path* to a reasonable number, not the number itself. Interviewers evaluate: structure (MECE chain), assumption quality (grounded, not wild), math (rounded, clean, narrated), and the sanity check.

## The core approaches

**Top-down:** start with a big, known number and cut it down by assumptions.
- US toothbrush market: 330M people × ~2.7 toothbrushes/person/year × ~$3/brush ≈ $2.7B.

**Bottom-up:** build from a small unit count.
- Coffee shops in London: ~9M people × ~x cups/day… or: high streets & stations & offices counts × shops each.

Use both when the case matters (market entry): the gap between top-down and bottom-up pinpoints your uncertainty. Choose top-down for broad national/global markets, bottom-up for granular/local ones — and say which one and why.

## The 4-step method

1. **Clarify scope first.** Annual or lifetime? Revenue or units? Which geography? Wholesale or retail price? This single step prevents most failures.
2. **Structure the tree** — write the equation before any numbers. Population → segments by age/behavior → frequency → units or price.
3. **Estimate with round numbers and stated anchors.** Use memorized anchors (US population ~330M, EU ~450M, US GDP ~$28T, average household ~2.5 people) and known reference points (e.g., smartphone penetration >80% among adults). Never use 0% or 100% of anything, and never pull a silent assumption.
4. **Compute aloud, then sanity-check.** "That implies every person over 65 buys two golf clubs a year — plausible upper bound; a more reasonable number is…" Adjust visibly and explain why.

## Key factors and considerations

- **Segmentation is the lever of credibility.** Demographics (age, income), behavior (frequency), usage classes (residential vs commercial) — the best sizings have 2–3 clean splits.
- **Triangulate** — segment estimates should roughly agree; if two paths disagree by 10×, reconcile aloud.
- **Per-capita anchors** — the difference between a great and okay answer is often just a couple of good anchors (e.g., $/person/year spending on vitamins).
- **Supply side as an alternative** — for constrained systems (airport capacity, hospital beds), size from capacity, not demand.
- **Replacement vs first-time** — for durables, split new buyers from replacements (multiply population × penetration / replacement-cycle years).

## Common pitfalls

1. **Doing math before structure** — the #1 fail; tree first, numbers second.
2. **Unchecked assumptions** — silent 0%/100%, or assuming "everyone" behaves like you ("5% of households own EVs based on my neighborhood").
3. **Messy math** — skip rounding and the arithmetic eats you; round hard, narrate clearly.
4. **No consistency check** — ending on a wildly implausible number without noticing.
5. **Recalculating instead of adapting** — interviewers will change parameters mid-case to test adaptability; hold the structure, swap the number.
6. **Confusing market size with attainable revenue** — sizing is the market; share, penetration, and conversion come after.

## Worked example (condensed)

**Prompt:** Size the US golf-ball market.

**Top-down:** 330M people. Golfers ≈ 25% of adults ever played, ~10% play regularly ≈ 25M regular players. Average regular player uses ~1.5 balls/round and plays ~20 rounds/year → 30 balls/year. Plus irregular players and practice ≈ +20%. Total ≈ 25M × 30 × 1.2 ≈ 900M balls/year. At ~$2.50/ball retail-equivalent → ~$2.25B market.

**Bottom-up check:** ~16,000 US golf courses × ~30,000 rounds/course/year × 1.5 balls lost/bought per round ≈ 720M balls — same order of magnitude, adds credibility.

**Sanity check:** "Close enough across methods — the market is on the order of $2B annually, consistent with a large sport but a niche industry."

## Practice drills

- Size the market for organic toothpaste in the US.
- How many bicycles are sold annually in Germany?
- What is the annual revenue of coffee shops in your city?
- How many pizzas are delivered in NYC on a Friday night?
- What's the market size for EV chargers in California by 2030?
