# Frameworks Overview: The Toolkit

Frameworks are thinking tools, not scripts. "The goal is not to memorize frameworks... the goal is to solve the client's problem" (Management Consulted). Interviewers at MBB explicitly reject candidates who recite memorized structures. What works: master 6–8 starting points deeply, then build a tailored tree for the case in front of you in the first 90 seconds.

Source: [CaseXcel – Consulting frameworks](https://www.casexcel.com/consulting-frameworks), [HackingTheCaseInterview – Frameworks guide](https://www.hackingthecaseinterview.com/pages/case-interview-frameworks), [Road to Offer – Complete framework guide](https://www.roadtooffer.com/blog/case-interview-frameworks-complete-guide)

## The map: which starting point fits which prompt

| Prompt signals | Starting framework | Key first question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Profits are declining / margins below target" | Profitability tree | Revenue side or cost side? |
| "Should we enter market X / country Y?" | Market entry (4 gates) | How large, growing, and contestable is the market? |
| "Should we acquire/merge with company Y?" | M&A (rationale → synergies → price) | What is the strategic rationale? |
| "How should we price this product / price change?" | Pricing triangle | What is the objective (profit, share, positioning)? |
| "How do we grow?" | Growth levers (organic vs inorganic) | Where does current revenue actually come from? |
| "How many X are there / what is the market worth?" | Market sizing (top-down or bottom-up) | Scope: annual? geography? unit of measure? |
| "Our costs are too high / process is slow / capacity constrained" | Operations & cost (cost baseline → bottleneck) | Which cost bucket or process stage moved? |
| "Should we launch this product / how do we launch?" | New product (market → need → capability → economics) | Go/no-go, launch design, or failed-launch recovery? |

## Framework vs structure — not the same thing

- **Framework** = the reusable lens you bring into the room (profitability, market entry, M&A…).
- **Structure** = the specific tree you build in the case, for that client, against that objective.

Interviewers score the structure, not the framework. Your job is to convert the starting lens into a tailored tree: rename buckets, add industry-specific sub-drivers, and embed a hypothesis in each branch.

## The TRIM test for any structure you present

Before you say a word, check your tree (HackingTheCaseInterview):

- **T**ailored — uses this client's specifics (industry, business model, situation)
- **R**elevant — every bucket serves the stated objective
- **I**nsight-driven — branches are hypotheses ("Is price erosion in the premium segment the driver?"), not topic labels ("Look at revenue")
- **M**ECE — mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive

## Classic frameworks to know (but not recite)

- **Porter's Five Forces** — industry attractiveness (rivalry, supplier power, buyer power, substitutes, entry barriers). Pull in the one or two forces that matter here, don't list all five.
- **4 Ps** — Product, Price, Place, Promotion. Use for marketing and go-to-market execution.
- **3 Cs / 5 Cs** — Customers, Competitors, Company (+ Collaborators, Context). Situation diagnosis before strategy.
- **BCG growth–share matrix** — invest vs harvest decisions across a portfolio (Stars, Cash Cows, Question Marks, Dogs).
- **Ansoff Matrix** — growth by product × market: penetration, product development, market development, diversification. A secondary lens inside growth cases — don't use it as your entire structure.
- **STP** — Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning. The bridge between situation analysis and marketing execution.
- **McKinsey 7S** — internal alignment (Strategy, Structure, Systems, Shared values, Style, Staff, Skills). Signal-strong but rarely the whole answer; mainly useful in organization/transformation cases.

## How to build a custom structure in under two minutes

1. **Listen for the core decision** in the first two sentences of the prompt. The type of decision tells you which starting lens fits.
2. **Clarify the objective** — profit? revenue? share? impact? confirm the metric of success.
3. **Sketch a 3–5 bucket tree** on the first-principles anatomy of the problem: for profit problems that's the profit identity; for decisions that's usually market/competition/capability/economics; for processes it's the flow itself.
4. **Inject hypotheses** into each branch and rename buckets in the client's language.
5. **Present in one sentence of overview, then the tree** — and then actually use it, signposting every transition.

## The most common framework mistake

**Forcing a memorable framework onto a problem it doesn't fit.** Interviewers have seen thousands of candidates do exactly this, and it flags a candidate as someone who memorized rather than thinks. When the prompt doesn't match any starting lens cleanly (true in most real interviews and nearly all final rounds), build from first principles: what decision does the client face, and what are the mutually exclusive dimensions on which that decision turns?
