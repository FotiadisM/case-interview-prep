# Valuation Cases (Extended)

Valuation questions appear mostly as components of M&A and PE cases ("What is this company worth?") and occasionally standalone ("Value a hair salon chain"). The consulting version never requires a full DCF build — you need the three simplified logics and the judgment of when to use them.

Source: [Road to Offer – M&A framework](https://www.roadtooffer.com/blog/m-and-a-case-framework), [Management Consulted – M&A valuation](https://managementconsulted.com/case-interview-frameworks-mergers-acquisitions/)

## The three approaches

| Method | Logic | When it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Comparable company multiples** | Apply peers' EV/EBITDA (or P/E, revenue multiple) to the target | When public peers exist; the default base case |
| **Precedent transactions** | What acquirers actually paid for similar companies | When control/premium context matters; pricing, not just value |
| **Simplified DCF** | Value = free cash flow ÷ (discount rate − growth) (perpetuity shorthand) | When you know cash-flow trajectory and want an intrinsic check |

Arithmetic to rehearse: 8–10x EV/EBITDA on $50M EBITDA = $400–500M; $40M FCF growing 3% at 10% discount ≈ $571M; peers' multiple × target metric; premium = (price − standalone value) ÷ standalone value.

## How the valuation flows inside a deal case

1. Establish the target's fundamentals (revenue, margins, growth, moat) — the valuation follows the story.
2. Triangulate: trading comps give a floor, precedents give a market-priced range including control, DCF logic gives an intrinsic check.
3. Place the price: compare the asked price to the range; the gap is the premium being paid.
4. Judge whether synergies (for strategic) or operational uplift (for PE) justify the premium — valuation without a value-creation view is arithmetic, not analysis.

## Pitfalls

- Applying trading multiples to an already-includes-control price — double counting the premium.
- Mixing revenue and EBITDA multiples across companies with different margin profiles.
- Over-engineered precision — case valuations are orders-of-magnitude plus judgment; the interviewer scores the logic, not the decimals.
- Forgetting that value is a range, not a point — always give a range.
