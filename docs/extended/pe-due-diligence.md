# PE Due Diligence Cases (Extended)

Private equity diligence cases ask you to play the consultant on a commercial due diligence (CDD): a PE fund is considering acquiring a company — advise on the investment thesis. They appear at all MBB firms (PE diligence is premium-rate work) and most often at Bain, given the Bain Capital heritage. Good news: they follow a highly predictable pattern.

Source: [HackingTheCaseInterview – PE case](https://www.hackingthecaseinterview.com/pages/private-equity-case-interview), [Road to Offer – PE due diligence framework](https://www.roadtooffer.com/blog/case-interview-types)

## Know what you are — and aren't — being asked to do

| | Consulting PE case | Actual PE modeling test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Format | 30–45 min live interview | 1–3 hour take-home/modeling |
| Focus | Strategic & commercial diligence | LBO model, IRR, debt structure |
| Math | Valuation multiples, simple return math, market sizing | Full Excel LBO |
| Output | Verbal recommendation | Investment memo / presentation |

In a consulting interview you will **never** be asked to build an LBO model — but you do need the investment-return logic.

## The core framework

1. **Market** — is this a good market? Size, growth drivers, cyclicality, fragmentation (roll-up potential?).
2. **Company** — what creates value here: growth prospects, margin improvement potential, cash flow quality, resilience (how COVID-proof/simple is the business?).
3. **Risks** — customer concentration, key-man risk, supplier power, tech disruption, recession sensitivity.
4. **Deal math** — entry price/EBITDA multiple vs exit multiple, leverage and debt service, equity growth, IRR/MOIC targets, exit paths (strategic sale, secondary buyout, IPO).
5. **Recommendation** — invest / don't, at what price ceiling, with what value-creation plan and exit thesis.

The refrain interviewers reward: *PE returns come from entry multiple discipline, EBITDA growth, and exit — size each bucket of value creation*. A deal works when: reasonable entry multiple + realistic operational uplift + plausible exit = target IRR (typically 2x money in 5 years ≈ 15% IRR, i.e., >2.5–3x gross return expectations at fund level given leverage).

## The math to rehearse

- **Simple IRR**: doubling in 3 years ≈ 26% IRR (Rule of 72: 72/26 ≈ 2.8); doubling in 5 years ≈ 15%.
- **Multiple math**: EBITDA × entry multiple = EV; leverage: 40–60% debt at ~6–8% cost; equity multiple on exit = exit EV − debt ÷ equity check.
- **Value-creation bridge**: EBITDA growth from volume/price/margin levers × exit multiple − entry multiple paid = equity multiple.
- **Rule of 72** for all compounding shortcuts.

## Key factors and considerations

- **The exit is the thesis** — in PE cases, the recommendation is only complete with: who buys this in 5 years and at what multiple.
- **Multiple arbitrage skepticism** — assuming exit multiple > entry multiple is the classic lazy assumption; price it down or justify it.
- **Leverage is both engine and risk** — debt amplifies returns and also kills overlevered deals in downturns.
- **Customer concentration and key-man risk** are real no-go items in diligence, not footnotes.
- **Free cash flow quality** — EBITDA is not cash; ask about capex intensity and working capital swings.

## Common pitfalls

1. **Treating it as an M&A strategic case** — the objective is fund returns, not synergies with the acquirer.
2. **Confirming the company is good without modeling the return** — "growing and profitable" ≠ "buy it."
3. **Silent exit story** — no recommendation is complete without the exit.
4. **Ignoring leverage** — PE math without capital structure is corporate M&A math.
5. **Falling for EBITDA cosmetics** — dig for what propagates (contracts, retention, margins' durability).

## Practice prompts

- A fund considers acquiring a veterinary clinic chain. Would you recommend it? (Classic Bain-flavored CDD.)
- Evaluate a packaging company being sold by another fund: what's your diligence plan?
- A dental-supply distributor wants to roll up regional players. Does the thesis hold?
- Growth-equity fund evaluates a SaaS company losing money but growing 80%. Due diligence priorities?
