# Revenue Growth Cases (Extended)

"Revenue growth" is sometimes listed separately from growth strategy: revenue growth cases focus narrowly on the top line — typically "revenue is flat/falling; how do we grow the top line?" — while growth strategy cases span revenue, profit, and share across organic and inorganic options. The two terms are used interchangeably in practice, so treat this page as the quantitative engine of growth: the revenue driver tree.

Source: [Road to Offer – Revenue growth case](https://www.roadtooffer.com/blog/revenue-growth-case-interview), [HackingTheCaseInterview – frameworks guide](https://www.hackingthecaseinterview.com/pages/case-interview-frameworks)

## The revenue driver tree

```
Revenue = Price × Volume
Volume = Customers × Purchase frequency × Units per purchase
```
and for business-model variants:

- **SaaS / subscriptions**: ARR = customers × ARPA; new logos + expansion (seats/tiers/modules) − churn.
- **Retail**: same-store sales = traffic × conversion × basket size; plus new stores and e-commerce.
- **B2B services**: clients × average engagement size × utilization/realization.

The discipline: **decompose to the line that moved** (churn? traffic? ARPA?) and fix the driver, rather than proposing "we should market more" everywhere.

## Step-by-step approach

1. Clarify the goal (revenue amount or % growth, over what period) and where revenue comes from today.
2. Build the driver tree for this business model; identify which driver is lagging vs peers/history (the diagnosis step — see [Growth Strategy](/core/growth-strategy) for the full organic/inorganic expansion).
3. Quantify each key lever's math — e.g., +2% traffic at flat conversion = $X; a −5% churn reduction = $Y ARR — with stated assumptions.
4. Watch for double counting: if one lever acquires customers and another upsells those same customers, don't count the revenue twice.
5. Recommend a sequenced driver plan with the numbers summed against the target, plus the execution risk of each lever (CAC payback, seasonality, sales-cycle length).

## Key factors and considerations

- **Growth ≠ profit** — a revenue lever that spends $2 of CAC for $1 of gross profit is a destroyer; filter levers on unit economics.
- **The cheapest revenue** lives in existing customers (expansion, churn reduction) — quantify it first in any recurring-revenue model.
- **Base-rate honesty** — levers stack additively on the *passive* base; forgetting the passive base overstates leverage.
- **Acquisition as a lever** — when organic drivers can't close the gap, the inorganic option is a legitimate branch, not a taboo (see [M&A](/core/mergers-acquisitions)).

## Common pitfalls

1. **Skipping the driver diagnosis** — proposing promos when churn is the actual wound.
2. **Double-counting revenue across levers** — a specific, embarrassing arithmetic error interviewers probe for.
3. **Only proposing organic levers** when the gap demands inorganic speed.
4. **No unit-economics filter** — revenue up, value down.

## Practice prompts

- A gym chain's revenue per member is falling. Diagnose and grow.
- An airline wants +15% passenger revenue without adding planes.
- A DTC brand's revenue growth decelerated from 40% to 8%. Why, and what now?
- A media company's subscription base is churning. Revenue plan?
- A bank branch network generates flat revenue. How do you grow it 5%/yr?
