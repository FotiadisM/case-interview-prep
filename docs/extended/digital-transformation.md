# Digital Transformation Cases (Extended)

Digital transformation cases — "our client wants to become digital" — have become common at McKinsey Digital, BCG X, and Bain. The dominant failure is solution-first thinking: jumping to "build a data lake" before establishing the problem the technology solves or whether the organization can run it. The interviewer is testing business judgment with a tech vocabulary, not engineering depth.

Source: [Road to Offer – Digital transformation](https://www.roadtooffer.com/blog/digital-transformation-case-interview), [HackingTheCaseInterview – Digital transformation](https://www.hackingthecaseinterview.com/pages/digital-transformation-case-interview)

## The 5-layer framework

1. **Strategic objective** — what is this transformation *for*? Four archetypes: cost reduction (automate the back office), revenue growth (digital channel), competitive defense (parity), business-model reinvention (platform shift). The archetype determines the success metric for the whole case.
2. **Current-state diagnosis** — technology infrastructure, data quality, process maturity, talent gaps. Transformation is adoption, not deployment — BCG's widely cited stat: ~70% of digital transformations fail to meet their goals, mostly from adoption failure, not technology.
3. **Initiative prioritization** — impact × feasibility 2x2: quick wins first (fast payback, low change burden), strategic bets in years 2–3.
4. **Build vs buy** — turns on three factors: strategic importance (build differentiators, buy commodities), time-to-market, and organizational capability — never on Year-1 cost alone.
5. **Change management** — coalition building, process-before-technology, and adoption metrics (usage, workflow embedding) vs deployment metrics (launched, installed).

Supporting lenses to name: **PPT** (People, Process, Technology — the three failure dimensions) and **ACT** (Ability, Cost, Time) for a single go/no-go technology decision.

## Quantifying the transformation

Put a number on the prize in four buckets — don't anchor on revenue alone:

- **Revenue & growth** — conversion lift, retention, pricing power, faster time-to-market.
- **Cost & efficiency** — automation savings, error reduction, lower cost to serve.
- **Customer experience** — friction removed, NPS, churn avoided.
- **Strategic optionality** — a data/API layer that unlocks future moves (may have no standalone ROI today; say so explicitly).

Then do the arithmetic out loud: process cost × volume × automation % = savings, minus build cost and change cost → payback.

## Step-by-step approach

1. Clarify the objective — which of the four archetypes is this? (It changes the metric and the horizon.)
2. Diagnose current state — where is the client genuinely behind (not just "everything")?
3. Prioritize initiatives on the 2x2 — quick wins first earns credibility.
4. Make build/buy calls with the three tests.
5. Plan adoption, not just deployment — who owns behavior change? Process redesign first, then technology.
6. Recommend with quantified impact, phasing, and adoption KPIs.

## Key factors and considerations

- **Adoption > deployment** — the case differentiator is asking how the workforce will actually use it.
- **Data quality precedes AI** — most grand AI ambitions die on messy data; say it.
- **Legacy integration cost** — the hidden budget and timeline killer.
- **Capability honesty** — transformation demands skills the client may not have; build vs buy applies to talent too.
- **Sequencing risk** — parallel big-bang transformations fail; recommend staged waves.

## Common pitfalls

1. **Solution-first thinking** — recommending technology before defining the problem.
2. **Deployment metrics instead of adoption metrics** — the interviewer will ask "how do you know it worked?"
3. **Ignoring change management** — the #1 real-world failure cause.
4. **Cost-only ROI** — missing revenue/CX/optionality buckets.
5. **Treating build vs buy as a cost question** — it's a strategy question.

## Practice prompts

- A traditional bank wants to beat neobanks at digital onboarding. Where to start?
- A manufacturer considers Industry 4.0 sensor rollout. Worth it?
- Your client can't serve customers digitally end-to-end. Design the roadmap.
- A retailer's e-commerce costs are growing faster than sales. Diagnose.
- Build vs buy: should an insurer build an AI claims engine or license one?
